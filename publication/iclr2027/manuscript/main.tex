\begin{abstract}
World-action models jointly predict actions, future observations, and values,
suggesting that test-time uncertainty or sample consensus could improve
closed-loop control. We investigate when these signals support a useful
decision, using a frozen Cosmos Policy on explicitly defined LIBERO-PRO
subsets. A six-controller, 199-configuration comparison finds no statistically
convincing improvement over joint max-value selection. A decoder-space medoid
study with stronger initial-pool controls matches max-value at 112/180
successes; shorter execution horizons do not improve average success.
Frozen-candidate experiments separate selection opportunity from stochastic
continuation: one third of 288 saved candidates change outcome across suffix
seeds, although a narrowly replicated pool exhibits persistent value
misranking. Feedback and RGB-based regrasp improve success in selected settings,
including a 27.5 percentage-point recovery gain on new initializations of
eight difficult cells, without established transfer to new cells. Finally,
paired observation-contract experiments identify 16 lost successes after
an extra regrasp releases an already coupled object. These results distinguish
state-risk detection, within-pool ranking, and intervention advantage.
They support a controlled diagnostic account of inference-time planning,
not a universally superior selector or a new benchmark state of the art.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
World-action models predict robot actions together with their possible
consequences \citep{cosmos_policy}. This suggests selecting the most valuable
candidate, penalizing uncertainty, or following a representative mode of the
generated distribution. Yet agreement can reflect a shared error, and
predicting a consequence accurately does not make that consequence desirable.
A score distinguishing easy from difficult scenes may not identify the best
action within one scene.

In out-of-distribution manipulation, failures can arise from poor ranking,
a candidate pool with no useful action, insufficient feedback, or harmful
recovery. Querying more often changes both information and action commitment.
Gathering diagnostic evidence through a physical probe can itself alter
contact. Improving one metric cannot isolate these mechanisms.

We investigate them using one frozen Cosmos Policy checkpoint and a series
of deployed-controller and saved-state experiments. Our empirical
contributions are (i) scoped comparisons of geometric and decoder consensus
with joint max-value, including fixed-index and horizon controls;
(ii) repeated-candidate diagnostics of opportunity and continuation
variability; and (iii) paired analysis of feedback and RGB recovery,
including intervention-induced harm. We retain unsuccessful methods and
replay limitations rather than pooling heterogeneous runs into one success rate.
The goal is a diagnostic account, not a claim that consensus or uncertainty
methods are generally ineffective.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{World models and selection.}
Diffusion Policy generates actions with receding-horizon execution
\citep{diffusion_policy}, while Diffuser generates trajectories for planning
\citep{diffuser}. TD-MPC2 and Dreamer study learned world models for control
\citep{tdmpc2,dreamerv3}. Here the backbone is frozen, and most inference
jointly generates actions, futures, and values. This differs from the full
autoregressive, rollout-finetuned planning system in the Cosmos paper.

Self-consistency aggregates language-model samples \citep{self_consistency}.
KeyStone uses geometric clustering and medoid selection, whereas KDPE
uses a density-based endpoint criterion \citep{keystone,kdpe}.
Consensus itself is not our novelty. Our small-population adaptations to
Cosmos differ from their backbones, action parameterizations, and budgets.

\paragraph{Failure monitoring and intervention.}
Flow-based VLA uncertainty has been studied with independently trained
velocity-field models and active fine-tuning \citep{flow_uq}, not merely
noise samples from one checkpoint. Sentinel separates temporal inconsistency
from lack of task progress \citep{sentinel}; Rewind-IL combines overlap
monitoring with recovery checkpoints \citep{rewind_il}. Thus, neither overlap
alarms nor detection followed by recovery are new ideas by themselves.
UNISafe and StressDream investigate safety filtering and failure-oriented
world-model use in distinct learning settings \citep{unisafe,stressdream}.
Visual alignment and memory offer other robustness mechanisms
\citep{kachaev_blind_vla,kachaev_mu_vla}. Our study asks what the tested frozen
model supports before attributing gains to an uncertainty formula.

\section{Problem and Evaluation Contracts}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Model and control.}
At physical time $t_q$, query $q$ receives real external/wrist RGB, robot
proprioception, and a T5 instruction embedding, denoted $x_q$.
Full simulator state $s_q$ is used only for replay and offline diagnostics.
Joint inference samples
\begin{equation}
 (A_q^{(i)},\widehat I_{q,T}^{(i)},\widehat p_{q,T}^{(i)},V_q^{(i)})
 \sim p_\theta(\cdot\mid x_q;\xi_i),\quad
 A_q^{(i)}\in\R^{T\times7},\quad i=1,\ldots,K.
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
The controller executes the first $H\le T=16$ actions of one candidate,
then obtains new real observations. Generated images do not replace actual
camera input at the next ordinary query. Native actions contain translation,
rotation-vector, and gripper commands. Nine-dimensional proprioception is
robot state, not object poses. Most experiments use joint-value best-of-$K$,
not the complete original autoregressive $a\to s'\to v$ planning protocol.

\paragraph{Benchmark and populations.}
We evaluate the LIBERO-PRO Object task suite under Object, Environment, and
Position perturbations \citep{libero,libero_pro}. A task suite and a
perturbation factor are different identifiers. Table~\ref{tab:protocol}
separates the main populations. Full-controller studies use five denoising
steps and 280 physical actions; settling is separate. Recovery consumes
the remaining action budget. These are explicit subsets and intervention
populations, not the full official benchmark.

\paragraph{Outcomes and inference.}
Success $Y$ is the environment's task predicate within budget. Failure
does not automatically mean a drop: timeout and incomplete placement differ.
Our local drop proxy is not an official LIBERO-Safety score.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MacroSR}_m=\frac13\sum_f\frac{\sum_eY_{m,f,e}}{n_f},\qquad
\mathrm{MicroSR}_m=\frac{\sum_{f,e}Y_{m,f,e}}{\sum_fn_f}.
\label{eq:sr}
\end{equation}
We report paired effects, rescues (control fails, method succeeds), and harms
(the converse). Intervals preserve study-specific task/init clusters and
factor weights (Appendix~\ref{app:stats}). Unadjusted intervals and corrected
tests are distinguished; repeated seeds are not independent tasks
\citep{rliable}. Post-hoc findings are not confirmatory tests.

\paragraph{Replay contracts.}
Initial simulator states match in the 199-configuration study, but initial
$K=4$ pools are bitwise identical in only approximately 31--46\% of comparisons.
That study compares deployed controllers, not selectors on perfectly fixed
pools. The later decoder study uses cached initial RGB, proprioception, and
candidates: all 180 comparison groups pass exact query-zero checks.
After a different action, fresh observations legitimately diverge.
Saved-state studies instead control snapshots, executed candidates, and
suffix seeds explicitly.

\section{Signals and Controllers}
\paragraph{Disagreement.}
Across samples of one query, we compute
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{h,d}=\sqrt{\frac1K\sum_i(a_{h,d}^{(i)}-\bar a_{h,d})^2},\quad
U_{\mathrm{first}}=\sqrt{\sum_{d=1}^{6}\sigma_{1,d}^2},\quad
U_V=\Std_i(V_i).
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The first-action statistic excludes the gripper. Internal disagreement among
repeated entries of one latent frame is a separate diagnostic, not an
independent ensemble. Appendix~\ref{app:metrics} specifies aggregation axes
and online availability. A candidate-specific penalty is $S_i=V_i-\lambda U_i$.
Subtracting the same pool-wide uncertainty from every candidate cannot
change the argmax; pool risk can instead inform a control-mode decision.

\paragraph{Geometric consensus.}
OSC-medoid integrates clipped translation commands and composes scaled
rotations into trajectory proxies, then combines translation, rotation
geodesic, and gripper-sign differences with temporal discount. These are
command proxies, not contact-aware future poses. For candidate distance $D$,
\begin{equation}
i_D=\argmin_i\frac1{K-1}\sum_{j\ne i}D(i,j),\qquad
i_V=\argmax_i V_i.
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
We execute a sampled chunk, not an average of incompatible modes. Controls
include raw medoid, KeyStone-style guarded clustering, and a KDPE-inspired
kernel on induced endpoints. Appendix~\ref{app:geometry} gives parameters.

\paragraph{Decoder medoid.}
We adapt an external decoder-token selection implementation
\citep{decoder_code} to Cosmos's tiled action latent. Hidden features come
from the last conditional denoising call before the output layer.
The 196 spatial patches are not action-time tokens. If $C_{p,h}$ counts
decoded action scalars associated with patch $p$ and action time $h$, use
\begin{equation}
w_p=\frac{\sum_hC_{p,h}\omega_h}{\sum_{p',h}C_{p',h}\omega_h},\qquad
D_Z(i,j)=\sum_pw_p[1-\cos(z_{i,p},z_{j,p})].
\label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}
Full weights give fourfold weight to actions 1--4 and unit weight to 5--16.
Prefix-H8 sets weights 9--16 to zero. Selection uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection}
with stable lowest-index ties. Fixed-index controls test seed preference,
and action medoid controls for representation.

\paragraph{Feedback and recovery.}
We distinguish committing a saved suffix, querying on the old observation
(stale), and querying on current observations (fresh). These controls separate
extra inference from fresh information. Recovery adds a physical
open--retreat--relocalize--approach--close--lift sequence, then resumes Cosmos.
A frozen RGB localizer and confidence/workspace/reach-miss gate enable
non-privileged execution. Probe variants move the end effector before deciding
whether to recover: their information-gathering action also changes contact.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Broad Selection Does Not Establish a Gain}
Six controllers on 199 configurations yield 1, 194 rollouts; one empty upstream
initialization asset is excluded uniformly. Table~\ref{tab:consensus} shows
factor and macro rates. OSC-medoid changes macro success by $-0.68$
percentage points (pp), interval $[-3.33,1.99]$, 3 rescues/6 harms.
KeyStone-style has the best point estimate, but $+1.00$ pp has interval
$[-2.01,4.33]$. Raw medoid ($-0.01$ pp) and KDPE endpoint ($-1.68$ pp) also
do not establish superiority. This concerns the tested subset and adaptations,
not the original methods under their published protocols.

the last column is successes out of 199. K1 is the single-sample control.}
\label{tab:consensus}
\centering\small\input{tables/consensus_scores.tex}
\end{table}

A residual-risk study illustrates the distinction between detection and
ranking. Across 200 states/800 candidate branches, global failure AUROC is 0.650
but within-pool ranking is 0.509. The score reduces measured future-proprio
error by 2.14\%, yet success changes 117/200 to 113/200.
Six replay-integrity deviations limit strict causal interpretation of this
historical study. Separately, a frozen-ranker test improves its offline
objective but gives 165/360 versus 164/360 closed-loop successes.
State-difficulty prediction does not ensure action-advantage prediction.

\subsection{Decoder Consensus, Seeds, and Execution Horizon}
The decoder campaign contains 720 main, 360 fixed-index, and 360 H8 rollouts.
Decoder medoid and max-value both succeed 112/180 times (62.22\%),
with 7 rescues/7 harms and paired macro interval $[-3.33,3.33]$ pp.
Two additional Environment successes are offset by Position losses.
Fixed-index controls achieve 110/180 and 109/180; differences from decoder
also have intervals including zero (Table~\ref{tab:decoder}).

The matched H8 subset has 120 configurations with 30/30/60 factor counts.
Equal-factor macro success is 65.00\% for max-value H16, 59.44\% for max H8,
57.78\% for full decoder H8, and 59.44\% for prefix decoder H8.
Principal paired intervals include zero (Appendix~\ref{app:h8}), and candidate

Within each of three fixed candidate seed groups, one index accounts for
81--84\% of decoder queries (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanisms}, left).
Forty of 180 episodes use one index throughout, although most are not constant.
This motivates new seed-bank and permutation controls before treating hidden
similarity as physical correctness. It does not prove seed identity caused
every observed outcome.

\subsection{Opportunity Is Conditional on the Continuation}
Eight saved candidates from each of 36 snapshots initially give 18/36 max-value
successes and 19/36 empirical-oracle successes; 17 pools contain no successful
tested branch. The empirical oracle is
\begin{equation}
O_K(s)=\max_i Y(s,A_i,\xi_{suffix}).
\end{equation}
It is conditional on its suffix, not a universal upper bound on attainable success.

Three additional continuation seeds change labels for 96 of 288 fixed candidates.
Six initially all-fail pools then contain a success. Choosing and evaluating
on the same repeats yields an optimistic 55.56\%; held-out-suffix selection
gives 40.74\%, equal to max-value. Outcome-based selection also needs holdout.

Persistent misranking exists locally. In one saved pool, two alternatives
each succeed 10/10 times on new suffixes while the max-value candidate
succeeds 1/10 (Holm-adjusted $p=0.01172$). These are two actions in one state,
not independent tasks. On eight neighboring pools, held-out-repeat selection
gives 40/80 versus 36/80, interval $[-3.75,18.75]$ pp. It still uses offline outcome
information; a transferable online verifier remains absent.

\subsection{Narrow Positive Feedback and RGB Recovery}
A shared-prefix Object/task 0 study branches at $t=72$, after eight actions
of a common query at $t=64$. Fresh requery versus committing the remaining
eight actions gives 64/100 versus 46/100 successes:
$+18$ pp, init-cluster interval $[4,32]$. The 100 branches contain 50 initializations
with two rollout seeds. This supports that intervention on that task,
not a universal time 72 schedule.

A separate 36-snapshot repeat-feedback study gives 41/108 fresh 8 successes,
44/108 open 16, and 45/108 stale 8. Fresh-minus-stale is $-3.70$ pp,
interval $[-20.51,9.72]$. Matched-$K$/continuity tests on 24 states give 15 open 16,
14 stale, and 13 for each fresh variant. More frequent observation alone is
not an established remedy.

RGB regrasp provides the strongest narrow improvement: on new initializations
of eight selected difficult Position cells, continuation succeeds 13/40 times
and RGB-triggered recovery 24/40, a $+27.5$ pp effect,
init-cluster interval $[12.5,42.5]$, 12 rescues/1 harm. Same-cell replication gives
6/40 versus 25/40. Transfer to seven other cells gives 26/35 versus 31/35,
interval $[-2.9,31.4]$ pp. These cells were not necessarily unseen during
localizer training; general transfer is not established.
A later router adds only two successes over full recovery (55/75 versus 53/75),
so the full system's gain cannot be assigned to the router.

\subsection{Observation Contracts Reveal Intervention-Induced Harm}
The latest recovery experiment reuses 96 prefixes from 12 selected Position cells,
with two suffixes and eight arms:1536 branches, 48 initclusters.
When the old gate declines recovery, a 3 action probe either opens the gripper
or preserves its previous command, followed by refresh only or extra regrasp.
Refresh-only arms still keep old-gate recovery on previously eligible states.
Oracle controls use privileged localization/workspace information.

The primary preserve-refresh-plus-regrasp arm succeeds 145/192 times versus
151/192 for old physical recovery: $-3.125$ pp, interval $[-6.25,0]$,
9 rescues/15 harms, Holm $p=1$. None of six predeclared pooled contrasts passes
Holm 0.05. Post-hoc preserve-only achieves 161/192, but its ten net successes
over physical recovery are concentrated in one cell; cluster-sign $p=0.09587$
does not establish transfer.

Adding regrasp after the matched preserve probe loses 16 successes and rescues
none, across eight prefixes and six initclusters. Before divergence, the
target moves 32.01 mm and the EEF 31.68 mm on average, with contact throughout
all three common probe steps. Regrasp opens the gripper near $t=76$;
drops occur at $t=82$--85 during the maneuver. Refresh-only succeeds in all 16.
The ground-truth motion diagnostic (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanisms}, right) is offline,
not an online RGB input. The paired effect is $-8.33$ pp,
interval $[-11.46,-5.21]$, while cluster-sign $p=0.03158$ becomes 0.18948 after
Holm. This is a localized audited mechanism, not sixteen independent
confirmations of a general safety claim.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Risk, choice, and intervention require distinct evidence. A detector can
separate scene difficulty without ranking actions. Consensus can identify a
likely but physically incorrect mode. Requery can interrupt a useful sequence,
and regrasp can destroy a useful contact. Intervention-specific advantage
should include the cost and harm of gathering information.

The study is limited to one frozen backbone in simulation. Selected cells,
reused prefixes, and few independent clusters limit generalization.
K, horizons, and perturbation levels differ across campaigns: cross-table SR
differences are not method effects. The broad selector study has imperfect
initial-pool matching. Narrow gains do not establish new-task or real-robot
transfer; oracles use unavailable information. Shared-server timings are not
controlled latency measurements. Repeated exploration requires new holdouts
to prevent selection bias. These constraints do not establish impossibility
for other uncertainty, consensus, or feedback methods.

\section{Conclusion}
Neither geometric nor decoder consensus establishes a broad improvement
over joint max-value for the tested frozen Cosmos Policy. Candidate repeats
reveal suffix-dependent labels and narrow persistent misranking. Feedback
and RGB recovery help in selected states, but extra intervention can cause
physical failure. The next priority is to predict intervention-specific
advantage and harm on new cluster-separated data while auditing proposal
coverage, ranking, information, and recovery separately.
\label{sec:main_end}

\section{Latent Representation and Metric Definitions}
\label{app:metrics}
\subsection{Decoding}
Normalized low-dimensional targets are repeated in designated latent frames.
The decoder configuration has latent shape $[1,16,9,28,28]$
(batch, channels, time, height, width). One action frame has 12544 scalars,
or 112 complete copies of a $16\times7$ chunk. Channel-height-width flattening,
not spatial action-time tokenization, defines the repeated vectors.
For $B^A\in\R^{R_A\times T\times D}$:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde A_{h,d}=\frac1{R_A}\sum_rB^A_{r,h,d},\qquad
A=\operatorname{denormalize}(\widetilde A).
\end{equation}
Proprio uses repeated 9 D vectors, value its repeated scalar frame, and future
RGB the VAE decoder. Trailing incomplete proprio vectors are excluded.
Copies within one generation are correlated, not independent model samples.

\subsection{Sample and Copy Axes}
Standard deviations use NumPy population normalization, $\texttt{ddof}=0$:
\begin{align}
U_{A,\mathrm{mean}}&=\frac1{TD}\sum_{h,d}\sigma_{h,d},&
U_{A,\mathrm{first}}&=\sqrt{\sum_{d=1}^{6}\sigma_{1,d}^2},\\
\bar V&=\frac1K\sum_iV_i,&U_{V,\mathrm{range}}&=\max_iV_i-\min_iV_i,\\
U_p&=\frac19\sum_d\Std_i(\widehat p_{i,d}),&
U_I&=\frac1{3HW}\sum_{u,v,c}\Std_i(\widehat I_i[u,v,c]).
\end{align}
NaN-aware value metrics use finite subsets. Future-proprio dispersion uses
normalized latent-derived values; image dispersion depends on pixel scale.
Internal metrics for a single sample are
\begin{align}
U^i_{A,\mathrm{copy}}&=\frac1{TD}\sum_{h,d}\Std_r B^A_{i,r,h,d},\\
U^i_{p,\mathrm{copy}}&=\frac19\sum_d\Std_r B^p_{i,r,d},\\
U^i_{V,\mathrm{element}}&=\Std_\ell(\operatorname{flat}(L_i^V)_\ell).
\end{align}
Latent first-action copy disagreement includes all 7 coordinates, unlike the
6D across-sample statistic. A mean-over-samples suffix averages over $i$;
episode std then aggregates across queries. These axes cannot be conflated.

Joint $V_i$ vary with different actions/futures. Conditional variance
$\operatorname{Var}(V\mid x,A_i)$ requires repeats holding action fixed.
A common penalty cannot change selection:
\begin{equation}
\argmax_i[V_i-\lambda U(x)]=\argmax_iV_i.
\end{equation}
The historical risk-overconfidence-value predictor averages available
training-standardized components
\begin{equation}
\{-z(U_{V,\mathrm{std}}),-z(U_{V,\mathrm{range}}),
-z(U_{V,\mathrm{element}}),-0.5z(\bar V)\}.
\end{equation}
Its name is not a theoretical definition: these signs favor higher risk
for lower dispersion and lower mean value. The denominator is the number
of available components. It is not a validated universal risk formula.

\subsection{Prediction Error and Leakage}
For a completely executed prediction horizon:
\begin{equation}
E_p=\|\widehat p_{t+T}-p_{t+T}\|_2,\qquad
E_I=\frac1{3HW}\|\widehat I_{t+T}-I_{t+T}\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
SSIM/LPIPS are optional, not guaranteed present in every campaign.
Proprio scales must match. A forecast for $t+16$ cannot be scored against $t+8$.
The last H8 campaign leaves these fields empty, as with early-terminal H16
chunks; missing is not zero. Post-execution errors inform the next query,
not an accident already occurring inside the executed chunk.
Whole-episode aggregates likewise use future information.
At H16, query 3 starts at 48 and ends at 64; an error at 64 cannot anticipate
a drop at 48--56. Variable H/recovery require actual saved timestamps.

Aligned overlap for generate 16/execute 8 is
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{overlap}}(q)=\sqrt{\frac1{8D}\sum_{h=1}^{8}\sum_d
(A_q[h,d]-A_{q-1}[h+8,d])^2}.
\end{equation}
Disagreement may be beneficial correction; consistency can accompany failure.
Endpoint images from consecutive queries usually target different times.
Failure is the positive classification label:
$\mathrm{TPR}=TP/(TP+FN)$, $\mathrm{TNR}=TN/(TN+FP)$;
balanced accuracy is their mean. Feature/threshold selection and normalization
belong inside training folds. Holding out seeds but not task/init does not
establish new-scene transfer. Event detection requires pre-event lead time.

\section{Controller Parameters and Decoder Layout}
\label{app:geometry}
For clipped translation $u_h$, rotation $\omega_h$, and gripper sign $b_h$:
\begin{align}
\bar p_h&=\sum_{\tau\le h}c_pu_\tau,&
\bar R_h&=\exp([c_r\omega_h]_\times)\bar R_{h-1},\quad\bar R_0=I,\\
\vartheta(R,S)&=\arccos\left(\clip\left(\frac{\tr(RS^\top)-1}{2},-1,1\right)\right).
\end{align}
The broad OSC distance is
\begin{equation}
D(i,j)=\frac{\sum_{h=1}^{16}\gamma^{h-1}
[\|\bar p_h^i-\bar p_h^j\|_2/c_p+
\lambda_r\vartheta(\bar R_h^i,\bar R_h^j)/c_r+
\lambda_g\ind(b_h^i\ne b_h^j)]}{\sum_{h=1}^{16}\gamma^{h-1}}.
\end{equation}
Frozen parameters: $c_p=.05,c_r=.5,\lambda_r=.5,\lambda_g=.25,\gamma=.95$,
zero alignment window. Command proxies are not true contact-aware poses.
Averaging distances over $K$ including zero diagonal only rescales the medoid
cost. OSC ties use larger value then smaller index, so selection is not
strictly value-free in ties. Raw medoid uses flattened native actions;
KeyStone-style adds guarded clustering; KDPE uses induced endpoints.

The decoder campaign's action control instead uses first 5 actions,
discount.9, translation norm/$\sqrt3$, rotation-angle/$\pi$ with weight.5,
and gripper-sign mismatch with weight.25. It is not the broad OSC control.
Native axis-angle commands are not continuous 6 D rotations.

Decoder capture uses final conditional forward, action index 4 of 9,
spatial patch 2 and temporal patch 1:196 vectors of dimension 2048.
Incidence follows the actual channel-height-width repeated action layout.
Cosine uses a $10^{-12}$ norm floor and clipped dot products.
No policy-weight updates or averaging of incompatible actions are performed.

Candidate groups are $(1,999,998),(2,997,996),(3,995,994)$, separate from env
seed, and reused at every query. Object/Environment cover 10 tasks, init 0, 1;
Position uses x 0.2/y 0.2, init 0. Sixty base configurations times 3 groups yield 180.
This is controlled fixed-seed sampling, not new iid noise per query.

\section{Prospective Tests, Not Additional Results}
Freeze contact-preserving refresh/recovery before new task/init/cell tests.
Compare continuation, old recovery, and matched probes; report SR, drops,
false interventions, and compute. A possible objective is
\begin{equation}
b^*(x)=\argmax_b[
\widehat{\Pr}(Y=1\mid x,b)-\lambda\widehat{\Pr}(\mathrm{harm}\mid x,b)
-\eta\,\operatorname{cost}(b)],
\end{equation}
where $b$ is continue, refresh, or recover. This is a direction, not an evaluated
new algorithm. It requires paired training branches, cluster-separated
calibration, and a frozen holdout.
Decoder refinements should first pass new seed-bank/permutation/fixed-index
controls. When pools lack useful actions, proposal expansion or grounding
should precede more reranking hyperparameter searches.
