\begin{abstract}
When a manipulation policy loses the physical conditions needed for progress,
another action sample may not suffice. We study whether a short, visually
grounded correction improves a frozen world-action policy beyond additional
observation and requery. Our controller combines a separately trained RGB
localizer, a calibrated geometry gate, and a physical regrasp maneuver;
the backbone remains frozen and recovery consumes the original action budget.
On eight development-selected LIBERO-PRO Position cells, three recorded
evaluations yield gains of 27.5, 47.5, and 39.1 percentage points over matched
eight-action continuation. The largest confirmation records 16/64 versus
41/64 successes, with a paired init-cluster 95\% interval of [29.7,48.4] points.
A matched ablation records 25/40 for full recovery versus 8/40 for retreat
and requery; timing controls show positive effects at three scheduled points.
The findings motivate physical state correction as a complement to generative
control, not a universal failure detector. A subsequent simulator-state audit
requires corrected-runtime replication of the scoped gains; a completed
corrected 199-case broad comparison finds no significant aggregate advantage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A visuomotor policy may continue issuing plausible commands after missing a
grasp. At that point, increasing the number of candidate actions need not
restore the physical precondition required for task completion. An alternative
is to correct the local interaction and then return control to the original
policy. We ask whether such a correction yields a reproducible improvement,
and whether its benefit exceeds that of simply obtaining another observation.

We investigate this question with a frozen world-action model,
Cosmos Policy \citep{cosmos_policy}, in LIBERO-PRO \citep{libero_pro}.
The intervention combines task-conditioned RGB localization, a geometry
eligibility gate, and a short Cartesian regrasp primitive. It does not change
the world-action model's weights or use simulator object poses online.
The perception module does require task-specific supervised calibration;
the complete system is therefore not training-free.

Our primary scope is a previously defined set of eight challenging,
calibration-familiar task--Position cells. This choice reflects the recovery
problem and the available calibrated localizer, not the removal of unsuccessful
evaluation episodes. Every initialization in each manifest is retained,
including failed interventions, gate-rejected cases, and baseline successes.
We distinguish this scope from calibration-unseen perturbations and from a
complete three-factor benchmark.

\paragraph{Research questions and contributions.}
We organize the study around three questions. \emph{Does physical recovery
help?} We report sizeable paired gains on a fixed, fully retained population.
\emph{Is the gain just another observation?} Matched continuation and
retreat-only controls separate the maneuver from its sensing component.
\emph{When is the maneuver applicable?} Timing, grounding, and broader-scene
controls separate geometric eligibility from expected intervention benefit.
The contribution is an empirical decomposition of recovery for a frozen
world-action policy, not a new backbone, the invention of regrasp, or a
general-purpose failure detector.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Action-chunked and world-action policies.}
Diffusion Policy uses generative action sequences for visuomotor control
\citep{diffusion_policy}. Cosmos Policy adapts a video model to predict
actions, future observations, and value \citep{cosmos_policy}.
We retain a frozen Cosmos checkpoint and its native action representation.
Our candidate selection uses values generated jointly with actions and futures;
it is not a reproduction of the paper's full autoregressive,
rollout-finetuned planning system.

\paragraph{Monitoring and recovery.}
Sentinel separates temporal inconsistency from lack of task progress
\citep{sentinel}. Rewind-IL combines overlap-based monitoring with
semantically verified recovery checkpoints \citep{rewind_il}.
Monitoring and recovery are therefore established ideas.
Our experiment isolates a particular physical regrasp maneuver and its
incremental terminal-success benefit under paired continuation controls.
Unlike checkpoint respawning, the tested correction spends physical actions
from the current reached state; it does not restore an earlier simulator
state as an online recovery action. Evaluation branching is a measurement
tool, not an ability available to the deployed controller.
We do not claim a head-to-head improvement over those systems, which use
different intervention and evaluation contracts.

\paragraph{Scope of robustness claims.}
LIBERO studies language-conditioned manipulation, and LIBERO-PRO introduces
perturbations beyond the nominal task configuration \citep{libero,libero_pro}.
A change in initial state, a change in task--perturbation cell, and a change
outside perception calibration are distinct generalization tests.
Our tables preserve these distinctions and report paired uncertainty
\citep{rliable}. Broader uncertainty and consensus explorations are documented
separately; they are not the main empirical claim of this paper.

\section{Problem and Controller}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Frozen World-Action Policy}
At query time $t_q$, the policy receives
\begin{equation}
x_q=(I^{\mathrm{ext}}_{t_q},I^{\mathrm{wrist}}_{t_q},p_{t_q},e_\ell),
\qquad
(A_q^{(i)},\widehat I_q^{(i)},\widehat p_q^{(i)},V_q^{(i)})
\sim p_\theta(\cdot\mid x_q;\xi_i),
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
where $p$ is nine-dimensional robot proprioception and $e_\ell$ is the
instruction embedding. Each action chunk $A_q^{(i)}\in\R^{16\times7}$
contains translation, rotation, and gripper commands. The controller selects
\begin{equation}
i_q^\star=\argmax_{i=1,\ldots,4} V_q^{(i)},\qquad
a_{t_q+h}=A_q^{(i_q^\star)}[h],\quad h=0,\ldots,H-1.
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
All evaluated continuations use $K=4$ and five action-denoising steps.
A new ordinary query receives actual camera observations, not predicted
future images. The common prefix executes $H=16$; matched intervention
branches resume with $H=8$.
A separate H16 baseline continues without the scheduled switch.
Changing the horizon is thus an explicit control, not an unreported feature
of the recovery method.

\subsection{Calibrated RGB Grounding}
\label{sec:grounding}
The recovery module uses external RGB and the target category parsed
from the instruction. The frozen artifact is a DeepLabV3--ResNet50 heatmap
localizer with object-specific height estimation, trained separately from
Cosmos on 388 calibration images from 194 groups.
Simulator-derived supervision and camera calibration are used during
development; online object position is inferred from RGB.
For the target heatmap $L_\phi(I,\ell)$, the implementation selects its maximum:
\begin{equation}
(\hat u,\hat v)=\argmax_{u,v}L_\phi(I,\ell)[v,u],\qquad
c=\max_{u,v}L_\phi(I,\ell)[v,u]-\min_{u,v}L_\phi(I,\ell)[v,u].
\label{eq:heatmap}
\end{equation}
Here $c$ is a heatmap score range, not a calibrated probability of successful
recovery. Height $\hat z$ uses a frozen object-specific route: a learned
global head, calibrated regression, or a calibration-set object median
(Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}).

Using camera intrinsics $K_c$ and camera-to-world transform $(R_c,b_c)$,
we intersect the ray with the estimated horizontal plane:
\begin{equation}
d=R_cK_c^{-1}[\hat u,\hat v,1]^\top,\qquad
\hat p^{\mathrm{obj}}=b_c+
\frac{\hat z-b_{c,z}}{d_z}d.
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
Invalid rays or nonfinite estimates reject the intervention.
Camera calibration and robot proprioception are available inputs; true
simulator object coordinates are not online inputs.

\subsection{Eligibility and Physical Recovery}
At scheduled assessment time $\tau$, eligibility is
\begin{equation}
G(x_\tau)=
\ind[\mathrm{finite}]\,
\ind[c\ge c_\ell]\,
\ind[\hat p^{\mathrm{obj}}\in\mathcal W_\ell]\,
\ind[0.08\le \|\hat p^{\mathrm{obj}}-p^{\mathrm{eef}}\|_2\le0.50].
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
Distances are in meters and $p^{\mathrm{eef}}$ is observed robot position.
Thresholds and workspaces are frozen from the earlier calibration/screening
stage. The lower distance bound is a heuristic reach-miss condition, not
a validated failure detector or proof that the gripper is empty.

When $G=1$, the controller opens the gripper and retreats upward, obtains
a fresh RGB localization, then approaches, descends, closes, and lifts.
A second guard checks the refreshed position before approach.
It omits the lower miss-distance bound but retains finite, confidence,
workspace, and reach checks. If it rejects, the corrected implementation
continues from the physically reached state, without rollback.
Table~\ref{tab:primitive} lists the fixed stages.

The confirmation attempts the intervention at most once.
Every physical action counts toward the original 280-step horizon, after
ten settling actions. Rejected initial gates resume matched continuation.
The main experiment uses historical $\tau=72$; timing controls use
$\tau\in\{56,72,88\}$. This is scheduled assessment with an observation-dependent
gate, not an event-driven timing algorithm.

\paragraph{Execution contract.}
The controller follows
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\pi_\theta^{H16}\ \longrightarrow\ x_\tau}_{\text{shared prefix}}
\ \longrightarrow\
\begin{cases}
\text{regrasp}(x_\tau)\ \longrightarrow\ \pi_\theta^{H8},&G(x_\tau)=1,\\
\pi_\theta^{H8},&G(x_\tau)=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:controller}
\end{equation}
The gate is evaluated once; after any executed movement, continuation uses
the reached state and remaining budget. Relocalization follows retreat,
so approach does not use a stale pre-retreat image.

\section{Paired Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
\paragraph{Primary population.}
The eight primary cells combine x0.2 with tasks 5, 6, and 9;
y0.2 with tasks 4, 6, and 9; and y0.3 with tasks 1 and 5.
They belong to the LIBERO-PRO Object task suite under Position perturbations.
They were selected during earlier development for difficult manipulation and
localizer applicability, not sampled uniformly from LIBERO-PRO.
All eight cells, including cells with no benefit, remain in every primary
aggregate. Appendix~\ref{app:cells} lists commands and final per-cell counts.

P3c tests init~35--39 after screening and development on other groups.
P3d uses init~40--44 in a later development-stage replication, not an
opened confirmatory transfer holdout. The latest confirmation uses init~25--32
with new frozen rollout seeds. These IDs are outside perception calibration
(init~5--24), but some were inspected in earlier policy research.
We call this a fresh-seed replication on fixed cells, not a globally untouched
holdout. The three evaluations have different roles and are not pooled.

\paragraph{Branches and controls.}
At each assessment, branches start from the same captured MuJoCo/controller
state and observations. H8 and recovery continuations use paired suffix seeds.
The latest H16 control is a full, uninterrupted baseline.
Timing snapshots come from that uninterrupted trajectory, not a trajectory
already changed by earlier recovery.
Success before assessment remains success in the corresponding arms.

P3d additionally compares full recovery with a trigger-matched
open--retreat--relocalize--requery control, omitting approach, grasp, and lift.
The latest confirmation also tests a preserve-only extension.
Appendix~\ref{app:audit} explains the historical fallback audit and why
snapshot equality is not independent full-trajectory replay.

\paragraph{Evidence versions.}
The scoped and timing measurements below predate a simulator-state repair.
An audit found omitted solver warmstart and sensor-cache state; a
\emph{scoped corrected-runtime replication is still required} before treating
the headline as confirmed under the repaired contract. We retain the original
counts and label them historical, rather than silently upgrading their status.
The broad comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:broad} has already been recollected
and audited with the corrected snapshot.

\paragraph{Endpoints.}
The primary endpoint is the environment's terminal task predicate within
budget. For method $m$, cell $c$, and initialization $j$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SR}_m=\frac1{|\mathcal C|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}
\frac1{n_c}\sum_jY^m_{cj},\qquad
\Delta=\frac1{|\mathcal C|}\sum_c\frac1{n_c}
\sum_j(Y^R_{cj}-Y^C_{cj}).
\label{eq:effect}
\end{equation}
Cell sizes are equal within each primary cohort, so cell-macro and pooled
SR coincide there. These are not Object/Environment/Position macro scores.
We report init-cluster bootstrap intervals within fixed cells,
rescues $N_{01}$, and harms $N_{10}$. For equal-sized paired samples,
\begin{equation}
\Delta=\frac{N_{01}-N_{10}}N
=(1-\mathrm{SR}_C)\Pr(Y^R=1\mid Y^C=0)
-\mathrm{SR}_C\Pr(Y^R=0\mid Y^C=1).
\label{eq:rescue}
\end{equation}
This decomposition is descriptive, not an online decision rule.
It shows why a high method SR alone is insufficient evidence of improvement.
Timing branches and suffix repeats do not create independent tasks.
Multiplicity and cost interpretation are in Appendix~\ref{app:stats}.

\subsection{The Gain Exceeds Retreat and Requery}
On the P3d replication population, H8 succeeds 6/40 times, retreat-only
8/40, and full recovery 25/40.
Full recovery gains 42.5 pp over retreat-only, with 17 full-only successes
and no retreat-only successes. This supports the contribution of the complete
object-directed maneuver, not any individual stage in isolation.
Across all 75 P3d cases, including seven other calibrated cells,
the counts are 32/75, 36/75, and 56/75.
Full-versus-retreat gains 26.67 pp, with 23 full-only and three retreat-only
successes. On the seven other cells alone, the contrast is smaller and uncertain;
Appendix~\ref{app:bounds} retains the complete cohort table.

An observation-triggered replacement is a separate method requiring evaluation.

\subsection{Grounding and Intervention Opportunity}
The benefit is conditional on the perception and manipulation regime.
Among initial gate-passing confirmation cases, median XY error is 1.12\,cm
in the primary cohort and 9.56\,cm on calibration-unseen x0.3.
Replacing predicted XY with ground truth in a separate transfer diagnostic
changes 0/16 to 3/16 successes: better grounding helps but is not sufficient.
Other calibrated cells give 31/35 versus 26/35 with an interval spanning zero;
x0.3 confirmation gives 1/64 versus 0/64. The broader v2 comparison also
finds no significant aggregate gain. These boundaries constrain the claim
to the studied calibrated regime; Appendix~\ref{app:bounds} retains all
transfer and preserve-only controls.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
A calibrated physical recovery option has a substantial recorded benefit
within a defined manipulation regime. The retreat ablation suggests
a benefit beyond observation frequency alone.
Neither the experiment nor the localizer identifies an optimal recovery
action for every state.

The gate checks geometry rather than grasp state or expected benefit.
An unnecessary release can destroy useful contact, and an object center
need not specify a good grasp point.
Future control should separate feasibility, grasp evidence, and intervention
advantage. Geometric feasibility $G(x)=1$ does not imply positive
continuation advantage
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_R(x)=\mathbb E[Y\mid\operatorname{do}(R),x]
              -\mathbb E[Y\mid\operatorname{do}(C),x]>0.
\label{eq:advantage}
\end{equation}
Here $Y$ is terminal success and $C$ the declared continuation control.
This is a target for future paired-branch learning, not an estimated online
component of our controller. A failure-probability score alone does not
identify whether the particular recovery action helps.

Primary cells were selected during development; the narrative focus was
chosen after examining a broader research program. Disclosing this and
retaining all outcomes does not retroactively preregister the whole project.
One backbone, task-specific perception calibration, known camera geometry,
scheduled assessment, and simulation limit the claim.
New-init replication does not establish unseen-object, arbitrary-background,
or real-robot transfer. Drop flags are not official LIBERO-Safety scores.
A separate completed 199-case three-factor evaluation finds no aggregate
advantage: recovery and H16 have macro-SR 54.10\% and 53.42\%, respectively
(paired difference +0.680 pp, 95\% CI $[-3.00,4.36]$).
This bounds, rather than extends, the calibrated-cell claim
(Appendix~\ref{app:broad}).

\section{Conclusion}
The recorded experiments identify visually grounded physical correction as
a promising complement to frozen generative control: 41/64 versus 16/64
successes on fixed calibrated cells, an advantage over retreat-and-requery,
and positive effects at three assessed times, without extra physical-action
budget. The corrected broad test establishes a limit, not universal transfer.
Completing scoped runtime replication is necessary for the strongest empirical
claim. Beyond that validation, the methodological opportunity is to predict
the benefit of a specific correction, rather than equate geometric
eligibility or predicted failure with a reason to intervene.

\subsection*{Ethics Statement}
All experiments are simulated. Improved success does not certify safety.
Recovery consumes budget and can cause harmful releases.
Simulator object labels are used for calibration and offline analysis,
not as an undeclared online input. Real-robot deployment requires independent
validation and safety precautions.

\section{Statistics and Cost Interpretation}
\label{app:stats}
The latest confirmation defines four primary contrasts: recovery versus H8
within primary cells; that contrast on x0.3; preserve-only versus recovery
across both cohorts; and recovery versus uninterrupted H16 across both cohorts.
Its frozen analyzer bootstraps init clusters within fixed cells and uses
cluster sign flips with Holm adjustment.
The headline reports effect and 95\% interval, not the analyzer's finite
Monte Carlo zero as an exact probability of zero.
The H16 contrast restricted to primary cells is descriptive, not a newly
preregistered primary contrast.

The confirmation has 128 paired cases and 512 main branches.
Timing at 56 and 88 adds 768 branches from the same underlying trajectories.
The 72 counts are shared with main, not counted again as independent evidence.
P3c/P3d's older discordance tests are not silently combined with the later
Holm family. Intervals condition on evaluated cells; adding suffix seeds
does not measure generalization over all LIBERO-PRO tasks.

Recovery adds at most 25 physical actions within 280 and uses auxiliary
perception inference. Failed episodes reaching 280 are not successful
completion times. Shared GPU wall times and reused candidate pools are
not controlled production-latency measurements.
Suffix-only safety trackers and whole-episode H16 trackers have different
exposure windows and must not be pooled.

\section{Common Three-Factor Evaluation}
\label{app:broad}
We completed a fresh paired collection on the same historically inspected
LIBERO-PRO support: 50 Object, 50 Environment and 99 Position cases,
five controllers each, 995 complete rollouts. This is not an untouched
task/init holdout. All controllers use the same initial snapshot per case,
280 physical steps, generated horizon 16, and five denoising steps.
Except K1, selection uses four jointly generated action/state/value candidates,
not autoregressive planning. Macro-SR gives equal weight to the three factors.

\label{tab:broad}
\centering\small\input{tables/recovery_broad.tex}
\end{table}

Recovery versus H16 yields nine rescues and six harms. Its +4.04 pp
Position gain is partly offset by -4.00 pp on Object; Environment gains
+2.00 pp without physical recovery and matches the H8 control.
The paired factor-macro difference is +0.680 pp, CI $[-3.00,4.36]$;
versus H8 it is -0.65 pp, CI $[-3.76,2.67]$.
Intervals use 5,000 factor-stratified task/init cluster bootstrap draws,
keeping Position levels together. Cluster sign-flip tests use 20,000 draws
with a plus-one correction and Holm correction over four prespecified
contrasts; all adjusted $p$-values equal one. These do not prove equivalence.
Logical query counts are 22.99 per episode for scheduled recovery and
13.86 for H16; cached runtimes are not standalone latency comparisons.

The exploratory event controller never executes recovery in these 199 cases.
Its localizer supports 159 targets; valid localization occurs in 70 cases,
a close-near grasp attempt in ten, and coincident attempt/miss evidence in
three. No case supplies persistent miss evidence. It therefore reproduces
H16 actions and outcomes, not a demonstrated successful adaptive controller.

An earlier, pre-repair seed replication passed its 12-rollout smoke but stopped at
33 complete cases after a no-intervention parity failure. In an unsupported
BBQ-sauce case both H16 and event succeeded, at steps 240 and 237; there was
no recovery event. Proprioceptive hashes first differ at query step 48,
image/candidate hashes at step 80 despite matching seeds. Subsequent fixed-action
CPU replay on both hosts reproduced divergence from omitted MuJoCo solver
warmstart state. A versioned repair saves the complete integration state and
robosuite sensor clocks/cache; exact continuation tests pass at multiple
snapshot times. The repaired version passes 18 closed-loop smoke rollouts
and a fresh 995-rollout collection on all 199 cases. All H16/event query-input
hashes and videos match in the absence of intervention. An additional artifact

\section{Implementation and Harm Audits}
\label{app:audit}
The historical P3c/P3d collector could reuse baseline after a rejected
post-retreat guard despite retreat having altered the state.
The audit found zero activations in these datasets, so this defect does
not explain their measured gains.
The later confirmation retains the real reached state after post-action
rejection. Historical snapshot equality verifies restored state, not
independent re-execution of the complete trajectory.

In two P3d x0.2/task~2 cases, baseline and retreat-only succeed, but full
regrasp produces a drop proxy during approach and later times out.
In a separate observation-contract study, extra regrasp after a common
preserve probe loses 16 successes with no rescues. These branches share eight
prefixes and six init clusters.
Offline tracking shows the object moving with the end effector before
the extra release. This is mechanism evidence about unnecessary intervention,
not 16 independent proofs of a general safety theorem or a tested contact veto.

Older statistical P3c/P3d runs did not record all rollout videos.
Later mechanism replays are illustrative, not replacement endpoint trajectories.
The latest confirmation stores complete two-camera episode videos.
Examples should include rescue and harm with case IDs and shared-prefix
timing, without changing the evaluation denominator.

\section{Supporting Feedback Study}
\label{app:feedback}
A separate historical shared-prefix experiment tests one real-observation
requery halfway through the fifth chunk on LIBERO-PRO Object task~0.
It uses 50 initializations with two rollout seeds each, 100 pairs, a shared
four-candidate pool at step 64, and identical selected actions through step 72.
The feedback branch samples a new H8 continuation from the real observation;
the commit branch executes the old suffix. Both then return to max-value H16.
The original experiment records 46/100 versus 64/100 successes, +18 pp,
init-cluster CI $[4,32]$, with 31 rescues and 13 harms.
Its original replay tolerance passed but does not certify the subsequently
identified missing runtime components; applicability of the v2 repair must
be audited separately. This is evidence about a particular feedback point,
not a universal query schedule, a regrasp ablation, or an independent
replication of the primary recovery cohort.

\section{Research Scope}
A separate research report retains candidate-selection, uncertainty,
decoder-consensus, and suffix-repeat studies. They address distinct questions
and populations and are not included as competing headline methods here.
All direct recovery controls, transfer boundaries, and implementation audits
remain in this manuscript. Qualitative exemplars illustrate mechanisms;
they neither redefine quantitative support nor substitute for paired endpoints.
